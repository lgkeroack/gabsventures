What it is

A portfolio that checks the weather

This is where my works live: a short list of projects, a few words about me and a way to get in touch. It’s set in EB Garamond and written by hand in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework and no build step.

It also keeps an eye on the sky over Squamish, British Columbia. Whatever’s happening outside plays out quietly behind the text, and the footer explains why: “It’s raining because it’s raining.” After sunset the site switches itself to dark mode, and on a clear night it draws the stars that are above Squamish at that minute.

Why it’s useful

Quick to load, easy to read

  • Nothing to wait for

    No framework, no bundle, no build step. Each page is plain HTML with its stylesheets, one script and one web font, so it loads quickly and anyone can see how it works with View Source.

  • Light or dark, your call

    Pick a theme with the switch in the corner and the site remembers it. Once the sun is down in Squamish it goes dark on its own, and the switch waits until sunrise.

  • Every project, explained

    Each work has a one-pager like this one: what it is, why it’s useful, how it works with real screenshots, and what it’s built with. Liftseeker is a good place to start.

  • The apps, one address away

    The Apps lives at apps.gabs.ventures: a grid of app icons, one click to each tool that’s live. It’s served from this same site, so there’s only one thing to keep up to date.

How it works

From a forecast to a sky

  1. 01

    Look out the window

    When a page loads, it asks Open-Meteo for the current conditions in Squamish and today’s sunrise and sunset. The answer is sorted into rain, snow, cloud or sun and kept in your browser for four hours, so the forecast isn’t asked again on every click. The footer says what it found.

  2. 02

    Paint the weather

    A canvas behind the text draws whatever it’s doing outside: 200 raindrops that splash where they land, 150 snowflakes that wobble as they fall, half a dozen slow clouds made of soft, overlapping puffs, or a warm glow in the corner with faint rays and drifting motes. The colours shift to suit light or dark mode.

  3. 03

    Turn out the lights

    Between sunset and sunrise the site goes dark. If the sky is clear, it works out where 91 of the brightest stars sit above Squamish at that moment and redraws them every minute: brighter stars are larger, the brightest glow, and all of them twinkle. The five naked-eye planets shine steadily, placed by a simplified orbit for each. On a wet or cloudy night you get the weather instead.

Under the hood

How it’s built

Front end
Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework and no build step
Type
EB Garamond from Google Fonts
Weather
Open-Meteo’s forecast for Squamish, drawn on canvas
Night sky
Star and planet positions worked out in the browser from a small catalogue and the time of day
Hosting
Vercel, with a small middleware that serves apps.gabs.ventures from the same project
