Infograph

A subset of a segment of a slice.

A small tool for picturing parts of a whole. Split a rectangle, nest one inside another, and label them until the picture matches what you mean.

What it is

Who are we actually talking about?

Conversations about users, customers or markets slide between groups without anyone noticing: everyone who visits, then the ones who sign up, then the ones who sign up on a phone. It’s hard to keep track of which slice of which whole is under discussion.

Infograph makes the slices visible. Start with the whole, split it in two, nest a subset inside a part, and keep going. Each rectangle names itself from where it sits, so the picture always says what fraction of what you’re looking at.

Why it’s useful

A picture everyone can point at

  • Labels that write themselves

    A split becomes “a half of” whatever it came from; type numbers and it switches to percentages. Nest a subset and the label follows it down.

  • Two moves, any shape

    Split divides a rectangle in two; subset nests a smaller one inside. Repeat up to six levels deep and the layout adapts to fit.

  • A table when you want one

    The data panel shows the same hierarchy as rows you can rename, split, merge or remove, for when it’s faster to edit than to click.

  • Ready for the slide deck

    Export the chart as PNG, JPEG or SVG and drop it into the document where the discussion is happening.

How it works

From one rectangle to many

  1. 01

    Start with the whole

    Every chart begins as one rectangle. Name it after the group you care about: people who visit the site, everyone in the room, all of last year’s orders.

  2. 02

    Split it, or nest a subset

    Tap any rectangle for its options. Split it to show two groups side by side, or add a subset to show a smaller group inside it. Type a label on the line in the middle.

Under the hood

How it’s built

Framework
Next.js and React, with the React Compiler on
Layout
A recursive tree of rectangles that measures itself and picks a horizontal or vertical split to suit its shape
Styling
Tailwind CSS, with a sixteen-colour palette that keeps neighbours from matching
Export
html-to-image, at three times screen resolution for PNG and JPEG
