Where should we fly today?

What it is

The morning forecast, already read

Deciding where to fly usually means a dozen open tabs: windgrams, station reports and model forecasts, one site at a time. Liftseeker does that reading for you.

It covers 223 launch sites, from the Kootenays and the Sea to Sky to the Prairies and Quebec, and ranks them for today and tomorrow. Open any site to see its forecast, the wind at altitude and the windgram the ranking came from.

Why it’s useful

Less research, more flying

  • One list instead of a dozen tabs

    Every site’s forecast is read and scored, so the decision starts with a ranking rather than a research project.

  • Ranked the way pilots think

    Sort by consistency (how strong the lift is, and for how long), by peak lift, or by how high you can expect to climb.

  • Its own five-day forecast

    A built-in soaring model works out thermal strength, thermal ceiling, maximum soarable height and cloud base, hour by hour, from Environment Canada’s high-resolution model.

  • Tells you when it’s on

    Star your sites and get a daily email when any of them meets the conditions you set. One more click opens Airplan to check the airspace above launch.

How it works

From a picture to a ranking

  1. 01

    Read the windgrams

    Each morning Liftseeker fetches every site’s windgram from CanadaRASP and reads the image itself: it finds the scale, measures the lift band and the top of the boundary layer, and turns a picture into numbers it can rank.

  2. 02

    Model the day

    For a longer view, a soaring engine works through the atmosphere above each site from the model data and charts thermal strength against the ceiling and cloud base for the next five days.

  3. 03

    Check the wind aloft

    Wind, temperature and cloud at launch and every 500 m above it, for the hours that matter, so a strong day that’s also a windy one doesn’t catch you out.

Under the hood

How it’s built

Front end
Next.js, TypeScript and Tailwind, with Chart.js for the forecasts
Forecasts
Environment Canada’s HRDPS model, decoded from GRIB2, with Open-Meteo as a fallback; windgrams from CanadaRASP
Accounts
Google, Facebook or email sign-in, Postgres through Prisma, email alerts through Resend
Hosting
